About

00 / PROFILE

Associate professor in New Media & Network Communication and Head of the Journalism Program. My academic path runs from Information Management and Information Systems (library and information science), through a master's in Computer Applications, into a PhD in Journalism & Communication — an interdisciplinary position combining information science, computation and communication research.

Research focuses on computational communication methods and crisis communication: two-stage LDA topic induction instead of dictionary matching, mapping 4,934 GDELT emergency-reporting items onto a four-dimension framework, then breakpoint comparison with significance tests. Data work runs in parallel across library circulation records (2021—2026) and public-opinion surveys.

Teaching covers four courses plus practicums and a course-designation project; in practice I mentor student teams in data-journalism competitions (national third prize in 2020, outstanding mentor at provincial level in 2024).

I treat tools as part of the research pipeline rather than as a talking point: reference verification, document layout and structured knowledge management are all reproducible workflows — results can be recomputed, materials can be handed over, and the site keeps itself current.
